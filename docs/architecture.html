How a shopper's photo becomes a try-on, and what keeps it safe

The phone never talks to the AI company directly. Our own small server sits in the middle and holds the three things that matter: the API key, the spending limits, and the privacy rules.

  1. 1The shopper taps Try on and takes or picks a photo. The phone shrinks it and strips GPS and device data.
  2. 2The app sends the photo and the product id to our server. It never sends a garment image or touches the AI key.
  3. 3The server checks the photo is a real image of a sane size, the product is one we allow, and that this shopper and the whole service are within today's limits. Bad requests stop here and cost nothing.
  4. 4The server sends the photo and our own garment photo to the AI service, and gives the app a signed, 15-minute ticket for this one job.
  5. 5The app checks in every 2 seconds. In about 10–20 seconds the try-on image comes back to the phone. Nothing is saved on our side, and the AI service deletes its copies on its own.

Privacy

  • We never store the photo: no database, no file storage, no logs.
  • The AI service deletes the photo when the job ends, and the result within 60 minutes.
  • The vendor does not train on our customers' images.
  • Closing the screen discards everything on the phone.

Open item: the vendor keeps request records (ids and times, no images). Their deletion should go in the contract.

Spend control

  • 5 try-ons per shopper per hour.
  • 25 per day for the whole service, a hard ceiling.
  • Only BRB products can be tried on.
  • Failed jobs are free, and we never auto-retry a paid call.
  • If the limit counter goes down, try-on pauses rather than spending blind.

No lock-in

The AI company sits behind one plug. Switching to another vendor, or to our own model later, means writing one new file on the server. The app does not change and shoppers notice nothing.

Slow or failing AI calls get a friendly message, a retry button, and a 2-minute cut-off.
